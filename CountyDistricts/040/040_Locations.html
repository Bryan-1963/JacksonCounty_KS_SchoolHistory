1881:

The Hoyt Schoolhouse was located on the east side of what is now S Road just over ½ mile north of 110 Road or just southeast of Burnett Street and Eastern Avenue (Ref 14). [39°15'11.0"N 95°42'02.7"W]. Note that the town of Hoyt did not yet exist in its current location and was only a Post Office on the north side of what is now 102 Road and ¼ mile east of S Road (Ref 14) [39°13'52.1"N 95°41'47.7"W].


Oct 13, 1881:

The Holton Recorder reported that the stone, sand and some of the lumber for the new Hoyt schoolhouse had been hauled and the contractor, Mr. Eggleston, would soon start work.


Jun 21, 1883:

The Holton Recorder reported that the Hoyt schoolhouse was “badly injured” in a storm that blew down two nearby houses and a barn.


1887:

The Hoyt Schoolhouse was located on the northwest corner of what is now the intersection of S Road and 118 Road (Ref 15) [39°15'36.7"N 95°42'04.9"W].


May 25, 1887:

The Holton Signal reported that the Hoyt schoolhouse was struck by lightning and slightly damaged. The Jackson County Federal ran a similar story on June 2, 1887.


Jun 11, 1896:

The Holton Recorder reported that a picnic would be held at Mrs. Lindsay's Grove, 1 mile east of the “Hoyt's new school house.” Note that in 1881 and 1903 the Lindsay land noted on the available maps would have been zero to ½ mile east of the school locations.


Oct 19, 1900:

The Tribune (Holton, Kansas newspaper) had an ad for R.A Dunahugh's farm sale located 1 mile east and 1 ½ mile south of Mayetta and 4 ½ miles north of the Hoyt school. This would exactly match the 1887 location of the schoolhouse but would also be close to the 1903 location.


1903:

The Hoyt Schoolhouse was located on the west side of Eastern Avenue (S Road) a little north of what is now 6th Street Terrace (Ref 12). This would be the northwest corner of Eastern and 7th if 7th went through [39°15'06.7"N 95°42'04.7"W].
