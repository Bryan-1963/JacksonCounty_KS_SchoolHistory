1869:

The Hoyt school district was organized. (Ref 89)


Jan 7, 1871:

The Jackson County Register of Deeds recorded that one acre was conveyed to District 40 for $5. It was located in the SW corner of the W 1/2 of the NW 1/4 of 24-9-15. In 1982 this location was the corner of land owned by John Marr. (Ref 89)


1882-1883:

The district had 21 pupils. The teacher was Mary Lindsay was paid $29 per month for a six month term. Some of the students were: Oskar Bainbridge, Maris Penry, Han Penry, John Penry, Will Penry, Elwood Penry, Mattie Penry, Eddie Penry, John Wallace, Robert Wallace, Charlie McKeage, Allace White, Lindsay Oliver, Charlie Oliver, Wilda Oliver, Clarence Oliver, Jessie Oliver, Ella Oliver, Frank Oliver (Ref 89).


Nov 23, 1894:

District 40 acquired land across the road on the NE corner of the SW 1/4 of 23-9-15. The tract was 12 rods east to west and 12 rods 10 feet from north to south. It was acquired from A.D. Walker and wife for $150. (Ref 89)


1908-1918:

Students attended a frame schoolhouse on the NE side of Hoyt. (Ref 89)


1908-1911:

The Hoyt school included two years of high school. (Ref 89)


1912-1917:

The high school term was increased to three years and the school year lasted nine months. (Ref 89)


1917:

There were no high school graduates due to a change in requirements and the class returned to complete a fourth year. (Ref 89)


1918:

There were 39 pupils and three teachers. Hoyt was classified as a Class D school. (Ref 89)


Apr 17, 1918:

Commencement exercises were held at the Hoyt M.E. Church and the speaker was governor Capper. (Ref 89)


May 23, 1918:

School closed out with a picnic at Woburn School with Professor Reed and Miss Weigtman in ..? (Ref 89)
