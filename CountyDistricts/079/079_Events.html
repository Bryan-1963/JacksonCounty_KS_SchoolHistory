Mar 4, 1887:

Preliminary notices for the formation of a new Mayetta school district were posted (Ref 71).


March 24, 1887:

District 79 was formed with the following boundaries (Ref 71):

Beginning with the NE corner of the NW ¼ of Section 14, township 8, range 15 thence south 2 miles, thence west 1 ½ miles, thence north 2 miles, thence east 1 ½ miles back to the place of beginning, containing 3 sections.


Apr 26, 1887:

A citizens meeting was held at the home of John Flynn to elect the Mayetta school district officers. Frank Ashton called the meeting to order and they elected John Flynn to be director, E.J. Lunger to be clerk and Frank Ashton to be treasurer (Ref 71).


May 7, 1887:

A public meeting was held for selecting a schoolhouse site. They debated a site at the north end of First Street and another location on Center Street. They chose the location on first street, but it was not unanimous (Ref 71).


Jun 2, 1887:

A second meeting was held to select a schoolhouse site and they unanimously selected lots 27, 29, 31 and 22 Jones Street, Lunger’s Addition (Ref 71). This location corresponds to the location of the school on the 1903 and later maps on the northeast corner of 2nd Street and Jones Street.


Jun 30, 1887:

The first district annual meeting was held in S.R. Jones’ store. S.R. Jones was elected as director, J.K. Latimer as clerk and C.C. Clark as treasurer. They voted to hold a seven month school year. The board voted to issue $1000 in bonds to purchase the site and build a school. The contract for building was let to Raisch & Binkley (Ref 71).


Aug 13, 1887:

The first schoolhouse was completed and turned over to the district (Ref 71).


1887/1888 School Year:

Carrie Naylor was the first teacher and was paid $40 per month (Ref 71).


1888:

The annual meeting voted to add a post and pole fence around the school and a contract was let for $39.


1888/1889 School Year:

School was held for ten months with two weeks of vacation (Ref 71).


1889/1890 School Year:

Teacher wages were reduced to $35 per month. The previous teacher agreed to the new salary but only taught for four months. Georgie Bell finished as teacher for the remainder of the year (Ref 71).


Jun 10, 1895:

A special meeting was called to consider a proposition to build an new, larger schoolhouse or make an addition to the existing building. They agreed to an addition and a bond election was held resulting in $600 worth of bonds. The addition was built and two teachers were hired for the upcoming school year (Ref 71).


1910:

Early in the year discussions began again for enlarging the school to accommodate the growing population. A bond election was called and $5200 was bonded for construction of a new school with a not to exceed cost of $12,000. A Mr. Neilson developed the plans for the building and Carter & Son of Lawrence, KS awarded the building contract. Lewis & Kitchin received the contract for heating and closet system (Ref 71).


Dec 19, 1910:

The building took a little over five months to complete and was 44x68 with a full basement and the students and teachers moved in to start classes.(Ref 71).


Sep 18, 1913:

The Mayetta Herald reported that the 42 tons of coal in the coal bin had caught fire. The coal was removed from the building and extinguished with no significant damage to the school.


Apr 24, 1918:

The Mayetta Herald reported that organization of a Mayetta Rural High School district was underway. Proposed boundaries had been determined and a poll of voters was underway.


Aug 21, 1918:

The Mayetta Herald reported that the school board had let a contract for a temporary one room building between the schoolhouse and the Christian church (to the east of the schoolhouse) for high school classroom space. They anticipated waiting for the end of World War One to construct a new, permanent high school. The high school teachers for this year were Professor Kelly, Miss Berkholder and Miss (Emma) Crabb.


Apr 16, 1919:

The Mayetta Herald reported that the annual Mayetta Rural High School district meeting was held. No change of the board members were made so George James continued as director, M. Fitzgerald as treasurer and W.M. Seibert as clerk. The present teaching staff was retained: Prof. Kelly at $1500 salary, Miss Burkholder and Miss (Emma) Crabb at $100 per month. The board was authorized to select a site for a new high school.


Apr 16, 1919:

The Mayetta Herald reported that the annual Mayetta city school board was held. The board was retained with no changes (F.L. Reynolds, J.M. Goheen, J.W. McMurray). Mrs. McCrory was retained as teacher with an increased salary, the remaining positions not yet filled.


Dec 24, 1919:

The Mayetta Herald reported that H.D. Hampel of Kansas City would be the architect of the new high school building and included their rendering of what it would look like.


Dec 30, 1920:

The Holton Recorder reported that the Rural High School District 5 would hold an auction on Jan 8, 1921 for the purchase and removal of the temporary rural high school building erected between the grade school and the Christian Church in 1918. The building was noted to be 22x32, 12 foot in height with a flat roof covered with 3 ply rubberoid.


1967?:

School Unification happened and Mayetta became part of USD337 Royal Valley.

This was the last school year that the building at 2nd and Jones Street would be used.


1971:

The Mayetta Elementary School moved to a new building on the east side of 4th Street (R Road) south of James Street. The building there was later expanded and eventually became Royal Valley Junior High School.
